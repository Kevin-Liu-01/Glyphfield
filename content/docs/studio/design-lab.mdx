Design Lab is Glyphfield's general composition workspace. It combines the shared Shader Library with a portable layer document, direct canvas manipulation, saved designs, deterministic still export, and browser-native GIF and MP4 capture.

The canvas itself is the output surface. The surrounding dotted workspace, library, inspector, zoom controls, and layer dock are editing chrome and never appear in an export.

On entry, the restored active artboard is fitted into view. Use **Fit canvas** to see all artboards, or open **Artboard guide** when you need help. View changes do not reposition your layers.

Open **Artboard setup** for shared presets or custom dimensions. The portrait row adds **3:4** (1080 × 1440), **2:3** (1080 × 1620), and **1:2** (1080 × 2160), alongside the existing Portrait (4:5) and Story (9:16) sizes. The selected dimensions are preserved in source, saved designs, and exports.

### Navigate multiple artboards

Click an inactive artboard's surface to select it, or drag that surface to reposition the board. On the active artboard, the first content click selects or edits its layer. The artboard header always provides a drag handle; double-clicking an inactive board enters it directly. Hold Space or use the middle mouse button to pan, including over content.

Click or drag the **Canvas map** to pan without moving artboards. **Center selected artboard** keeps the current zoom; **Fit canvas** shows all artboards. Selecting a board does not unexpectedly reframe the workspace.

## Add and arrange layers

Use the layer actions to add any number of:

- **Text** layers with editable copy, font role, weight, size, tracking, line height, alignment, color, opacity, and optional text effect.
- **Mark** layers from the active identity, including alternate uploaded or converted brand assets.
- **Image** layers from authorized local files or the identity asset library.
- **Shader** layers from the shared material catalog.
- **Effect** layers that convert the visible composition beneath them.

Select a layer on the canvas or in the bottom dock. Drag it to move, pull the resize handle to scale, use arrow keys to nudge, and use Shift + arrow for larger steps. The inspector controls exact position, size, rotation, opacity, visibility, material, appearance, and type settings.

The dock is ordered **front to back**. Move layers up or down, duplicate them, hide them without deleting them, or remove them. Multi-selection and groups let related layers move as one unit while preserving individual settings.

Use **Action history** in the canvas toolbar to inspect edits and undo or redo them. Command/Ctrl + Z works while the canvas has keyboard focus or the pointer is over it; Shift + Command/Ctrl + Z redoes. Text fields keep their native text undo.

### Layer opacity and blending

Every layer carries its own opacity. Shader layers also expose `normal`, `screen`, `overlay`, and `multiply` blending. Content-bound shaders contribute their own opacity in addition to the text, logo, or image opacity, so a material can be faded without making the underlying content disappear from the document.

## Text and text effects

Text is a first-class canvas element, not a single caption slot. Add up to 32 text layers through an agent-generated sequence and as many as the interactive composition can reasonably render.

Text can be rendered as:

- A solid identity color.
- A live shader clipped to the glyph shape.
- Bayer, ASCII, halftone, or posterized content through an effect layer.
- A sticker with a contrasting inner separation edge, cut border, laminate, relief, and shadow.
- A shadowed or outlined display layer without changing the original font asset.

Converter foreground and background colors are explicit. This prevents the converted glyph field from becoming accidentally transparent against the artboard.

Text and image selection boxes do not silently auto-fit their contents. Preview and export preserve visible overflow, including text in very narrow or short boxes, so the downloaded composition agrees with the canvas.

## Composition converters

Converters affect all visible layers below their position in the stack.

| Converter | Best for | Main controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bayer | Ordered print texture and stable pixel patterns | Cell size, threshold, contrast, inversion, foreground/background |
| ASCII | Rebuilding luminance from a fixed glyph ramp | Glyph cell size, threshold, contrast, inversion, colors |
| Halftone | Editorial dot screens and printed type | Dot cell size, threshold, contrast, inversion, colors |
| Posterize | Limited-tone fields with graphic color steps | Cell size, color levels, contrast, inversion, colors |

Effect previews render the actual converter pattern in the layer dock. During continuous slider movement, the preview uses a lower-cost intermediate render and commits the full result when input settles.

## Shader Library

The left library is shared with Animation. Search by material or source and filter by Fluid, Light, Metal, Gradient, or Graphic. Cards use quality-ranked previews; static thumbnails keep the full catalog visible while live previews are budgeted to the visible region.

Select a card to add or replace a shader layer. Common controls include three-color palettes, center, frequency, detail, strength, amplitude, density, grain, rotation, motion speed, opacity, blend mode, and shader size.

### Shader size and captured frames

**Shader size** behaves like material zoom from `0.1×` to `10×`. Values below `1×` reveal more of the field; values above `1×` move into its details. The control maps logarithmically so equal travel feels like equal visual zoom rather than a jump through the high end.

Live motion, choosing a still, and exporting a clip are separate operations. Live shaders use native display-timed playback; the time explorer samples editable shader time rather than replaying a recorded video. Its initial browsing range covers 30 seconds (or the restored time); extend it explicitly in 30-second steps to explore farther. Playback continues to show elapsed time without growing the range or adding stored frames. A finite browsing range is not proof that the shader repeats at its endpoints.

**Pause** stops at the current native frame without encoding a PNG or writing captured-frame assets. **Play** continues from the retained renderer. Pausing and scrubbing are editing operations, not requests to create saved appearance checkpoints.

Choose **Capture shader frame** to preserve the pixels that are actually rendered. Glyphfield keeps two pieces together: a lossless PNG appearance checkpoint and the editable material recipe with its native engine frame. Saved and duplicated captures display that PNG, so reopening a design does not depend on reconstructing precisely the same GPU pixels on another device. The PNG is stored once as a content-addressed IndexedDB Blob; portable source embeds it in `CanvasDocument.assets`. A library thumbnail is still only a representative preview, never the saved appearance. If a saved PNG cannot be loaded, its preview stays visibly unavailable rather than starting a different live shader.

Time seeking pauses native motion and updates providers that support direct time selection. Paper materials, original procedural shaders, and Prismatic Sphere expose editable time; static materials do not need a motion range. **Fluid Energy is stateful:** its appearance depends on earlier simulation and pointer input. You can pause its mounted simulation or capture its exact still, but a timestamp or PNG cannot replay its simulation history. Playing from a saved PNG starts a new simulation.

Most animated materials run continuously without a verified short repeat period. Prismatic Sphere exposes a configured loop duration, and some Paper presets have analytically plausible periods; neither is a pixel-verified seamless-export promise. Read a material's `motion` record from `/api/materials` or use `design.motion.describe` for the current composition.

Live shaders target smooth 60 FPS independently of export FPS or the number of chosen stills. Adding stored frames does not make playback smoother. Actual cadence depends on the device and composition; hidden or paused renderers stop drawing. Captures are made on demand, not encoded or persisted on every animation frame.

## Shader-cut sequences

Sequence mode turns one canvas shader into an 8–12 material timeline. Intro cuts can accelerate toward the final selected shader or use even timing; the last material holds for 3–6 seconds.

The timeline is deterministic: it records material IDs, segment start/end times, target shader layer, sequence offset, and paused frame. Preview, GIF, and MP4 resolve from the same timeline.

Stop a shader-sequence preview before saving, copying, exploring a frame, or exporting. The preview temporarily substitutes materials; it is not the saved composition. This prevents a transient sequence shader from being mistaken for the artboard's captured appearance.

Use sequence export for a deliberately edited montage. Use standard GIF or MP4 when the current shader should animate continuously without material cuts.

Sequences are optional: you do not need one to save a shader look. **Open Animation Studio** opens a separate workspace; it does not automatically transfer this composition.

## Stickers and surfaces

Any logo, text, or imported image can become a sticker. Sticker controls add:

- A cut border with editable color and width.
- A darker or contrasting inner edge so white artwork remains legible on white vinyl.
- Bevel, relief, seam, inset, foil, glint, laminate, and texture controls.
- Independent opacity, rotation, and placement.

A sticker does not require a surface. Choose no background and export it over the artboard color, or combine it with a cloth, metal, paper, glass, wood, stone, or live-shader layer. Surface textures remain ordinary layers and can be hidden or removed.

## Saved designs and recovery

**Save** stores the complete versioned canvas document in IndexedDB for the active project and tool. A saved design includes:

- Canvas dimensions, ratio, background, and workspace view.
- Every layer's ID, type, name, geometry, rotation, visibility, opacity, and blend mode.
- Text content and typography.
- Embedded or referenced assets.
- Layer order, groups, and content-bound shader assignments.
- Shader settings, captured PNG asset references, native engine frame state, shader sequence, and timeline time.
- Export width, duration, frame rate, quality, and loop mode.

Autosave preserves your working draft while editing. **Save** updates the selected named checkpoint, or creates one when none is selected. **Fork** captures the current edits in a new checkpoint linked to its parent; **Clone** creates an independent copy. Neither changes the original checkpoint. Restoring a checkpoint replaces the working composition with its stored source.

**Unsaved changes** means the working draft differs from the selected named checkpoint; draft recovery can still be up to date. Renaming changes only the checkpoint name, not its stored artwork. You can rename and then click **Save** to commit both the name and current composition.

The draft indicator shows pending changes as **Saving**. Fast tool/project switches retain queued edits. If loading fails, saving stays suspended while recovery retries so the initial canvas cannot overwrite your previous design. A local recovery journal can retain work while IndexedDB is temporarily unavailable; clearing browser site data still removes local work, so export portable source for an independent backup.

## Export

| Format | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| PNG | Lossless still with alpha when the composition supports it |
| JPG | Smaller opaque still using the selected background color |
| GIF | Animated browser capture with global/per-frame palette control and optional seamless closure |
| MP4 | Browser-native video, with quality selection and H.264 when the browser exposes it |
| Project file | Editable `.glyphfield.json` containing all artboards, embedded assets, and saved shader frames |

Choose **Project file** to share an editable workspace, then use **Open project** in Design Lab to reopen it. This uses the existing CanvasDocument format and does not require a PNG render. See [Project files](/docs/studio/project-files) for included data and portability limits.

Output width ranges from 320–3840 pixels and preserves the active artboard's aspect ratio, including portrait presets and custom dimensions. Motion exports use 12, 15, 24, or 30 FPS and a bounded duration. The export review displays the completed artifact before download.

Export waits for material readiness as well as fonts and images. Prismatic sphere needs its environment lighting to load; a loading silhouette is not accepted as a finished shader frame. If those resources cannot load, export reports a readiness timeout instead of silently treating the placeholder as the result.

Pending inline text edits are committed before export captures the composition; you do not need an extra blur click to include the words you just typed.

PNG/JPG freezes the frame you are viewing before rendering the complete composition at the selected output dimensions. GIF/MP4 explicitly samples motion from the current frame anchor and restores that paused pose afterward. Exports leave the canvas paused and do not add captured-frame assets to IndexedDB; use **Play** when you want motion again, or **Capture shader frame** when you want a durable appearance checkpoint in source.

Choose **Blended loop** to crossfade the recorded tail into the head and verify the closing frame. This edits the clip's ending; it does not establish a native repeat period for the shader. **Raw motion** keeps the unmodified clip and may show a seam when repeated. Stateful Fluid is excluded from automatic sampled intro cuts; it supports exact PNG capture, but timestamp-sampled motion export is unavailable rather than inventing a replay of its simulation.

## Source document

The Code drawer exposes Design Lab source version 4. It is a portable `CanvasDocument` envelope with Design Lab metadata for export settings, groups, layer shaders, ratio, shader sequence, timeline, and workspace.

```json
{
  "schemaVersion": 2,
  "brandId": "gt",
  "capabilities": ["animation", "assets", "constraints", "guides", "history", "layers", "pages", "text"],
  "pages": {},
  "elements": {},
  "assets": {},
  "metadata": {
    "tool": "design-lab",
    "designLab": {
      "sourceVersion": 4,
      "ratio": "wide",
      "exportSettings": {},
      "groups": [],
      "layerShaders": {},
      "shaderSequence": {},
      "timeline": {},
      "workspace": {}
    }
  }
}
```

Start with `window.glyphfield.studio.readSource()` rather than constructing the complete document from memory. Apply changes with `await window.glyphfield.studio.applySource(document)`, then inspect and export through `design.export`.

See [Browser API](/docs/reference/browser-api), [Shader Library](/docs/studio/shader-library), and [Formats and portability](/docs/artifacts/formats-and-portability).
